# Crime, Deprivation and Asylum-Seeker Support Maps

This repository contains infographic maps and small data tables exploring:

1. asylum-seeker support caseloads by selected English local authority;
2. national asylum-claimant nationality profile;
3. local recorded crime-rate context;
4. deprivation context;
5. national reoffending-risk indicators.

## Core framing

These graphics show **co-location and context, not causation**.

They should not be read as evidence that asylum seekers, migrants, foreign nationals, or any demographic group causes local crime.

## Most important distinction

**Supported asylum seekers are not the same as illegal migrants.**

- A supported asylum seeker is someone in the Home Office asylum-support system.
- Illegal or irregular migration describes entry or residence without permission.
- A person may arrive irregularly and then claim asylum, but the two categories are not identical.
- Nationality is not ethnicity.

## Why there is no clean illegal-migrant crime chart

Official UK crime statistics do not provide a clean local dataset for crimes committed by “illegal migrants”.

The Office for National Statistics has stated that it does not hold breakdowns of crime by asylum seeker or migrant status. This means any chart claiming to show “illegal migrant crime by area” would be making assumptions rather than using an official offender-status dataset.

The closest available official datasets used here are:

- Home Office asylum-support data by local authority;
- national asylum claimant nationality profiles;
- Police.uk / ONS local recorded crime-rate tables;
- English Indices of Deprivation / local authority summaries;
- Ministry of Justice proven reoffending statistics.

## Suggested wording

> This analysis compares local asylum-support caseloads, deprivation context and recorded crime-rate context across selected English local authorities. It shows co-location and context only. It does not establish causation or identify offender immigration status.
