# Why “illegal migrant crime” is not shown as an official category

This repository deliberately avoids creating a chart labelled “illegal migrant crime” because there is no clean official UK local-authority dataset that identifies crimes, arrests, charges or convictions by illegal/irregular migrant status.

## Key points

1. **Asylum seeker status and illegal/irregular migration status are different.**
   - Asylum seeker = someone has made a legal protection claim.
   - Illegal/irregular migration = entry or residence without permission.
   - The categories can overlap, but they are not the same.

2. **Supported asylum seekers are official caseload data.**
   - The Home Office publishes supported asylum seekers by local authority.
   - This is not the same as an estimate of everyone in the UK without lawful status.

3. **Crime data does not reliably identify illegal migrant status.**
   - ONS has stated that it does not hold breakdowns of crime by asylum seeker or migrant status.
   - Police-recorded crime data is area-level offence data, not a dataset of offender immigration status.

4. **Nationality is not ethnicity.**
   - National claimant nationality profiles can be shown, but they should not be treated as ethnicity data or as local-authority-specific composition unless the table explicitly says so.

## Safe interpretation

Use:

> “local crime-rate context”

Do not use:

> “illegal migrant crime rate”

unless an official offender-level dataset with immigration-status linkage is available.

## Strongest research question

A better model would test whether any local association remains after controlling for:

- deprivation;
- population density;
- age and sex structure;
- unemployment;
- housing instability;
- transport hubs;
- nightlife and retail density;
- policing/reporting variation;
- repeat offending.
